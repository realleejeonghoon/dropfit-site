Uploading client files to an online converter? Convert them on your Mac instead

November 2026 · macOS 14 or later

Online converters usually upload your file to their server, convert it there, and have you download the result. If you’ve agreed not to share client work outside, or the files are unreleased, it’s safer not to upload them. On a Mac, Finder Quick Actions → Convert Image and Preview can convert to JPEG or PNG, shrink, and combine files into a PDF without uploading anything.

You have to send 30 photos of an unreleased product to the ad agency today. The files you got are a mix of HEIC and PNG, and the agency wants JPEG. A search puts converter sites at the top. You’re about to click Upload, then stop: the contract had a line about not sharing files with third parties.

This happens a lot to freelance designers delivering brand work, marketers handling ads before launch, and studios sorting shoots with clients’ faces in them. For one or two files you can reopen your design app and export again. With dozens of files and a deadline, uploading them all starts to look tempting.

Where does a file go when you upload it?

Online converters usually send the file to a server, process it there, and let you download the result. Many say they delete files a few hours after processing, but how long they keep them and where their servers are differs by service and is spelled out in each site’s privacy policy. If you’ve signed an NDA with your client, check it before you upload.

1. Convert and resize (Finder Quick Actions)

  1. In Finder, select all the photos you want to convert.
  2. Control-click and choose Quick Actions → Convert Image.
  3. Pick a format (JPEG, PNG, or HEIF) and an image size (Small, Medium, Large, or Actual Size), then click Convert.

The new files appear in the same folder, and the originals stay as they were. Turn off Preserve Metadata to leave out details like location and camera.

2. Export at a chosen quality (Preview)

  1. Open the photos in Preview. Select them all first if there are several.
  2. Select all in the sidebar, then choose File → Export Selected Images.
  3. Set the format and the Quality slider, and choose a folder.

If the place you’re uploading to has a size limit, expect a few rounds of moving the slider, saving, and checking the file size.

3. Combine mockups or photos into one PDF (Finder Quick Actions)

Select the files in Finder, Control-click, and choose Quick Actions → Create PDF. One PDF appears in the same folder. Pages follow the file names, so numbering them 01, 02 keeps the order you want.

Before you send

  • Decide whether location and camera details can go along. If not, turn off Preserve Metadata in step 1.
  • Put the client name and a version in the file name, so a “can you resend it?” request doesn’t get confusing.
  • Keep the original folder separate and send only the converted files, zipped.

FAQ

Do converter sites delete the files I upload?

It depends on the service. Many say they delete files some time after processing, but how long they keep them and which country their servers are in is in each site’s privacy policy.

Can I convert on a Mac with no internet access?

Yes. Finder Quick Actions and Preview work offline. Dropfit doesn’t need the internet to convert either.

Can I remove location data from photos before sending?

Turn off Preserve Metadata in Finder’s Convert Image quick action before converting. The originals keep their data.

Does Dropfit send my files to a server?

No. All conversion happens on your Mac, and files and file names are never sent anywhere. It sends only usage counts when checking for updates, and you can turn that off in Settings.
