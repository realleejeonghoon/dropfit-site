Portfolio and application PDFs with a size limit: merge into one file under 50 MB on Mac

November 2026 · macOS 14 or later

University applications and portfolios usually come with a file count and size limit, such as "one PDF, 50 MB or less." On a Mac, scan the documents, merge them into one PDF in the required order, then bring that file under the limit. Dropfit for Mac does both steps at once: drop the documents on a PDF preset with the size set, choose the order, and it saves one PDF under that size. Merging is free; fitting a size is part of Dropfit Pro.

More early-admission, transfer and overseas-applicant tracks at Korean universities now take documents online instead of by mail. Ten scanned pages quickly reach tens of megabytes, and the upload field takes a single file. When "file too large" appears right before the deadline, you merge and shrink all over again.

First: what the admission guide actually says

Limits differ by school and track and change every year. Here is the wording from two 2027 admission guides, in translation.

  • Sangmyung University, 2027 early admission guide: scan every original document "with a scanner" and save them as "one PDF file (50 MB or less)" for online submission. Every document goes into that single file.
  • Soongsil University, 2027 overseas Korean and foreign-educated applicants guide: documents are grouped by category, in order, and uploaded as four PDFs. Eligibility documents: "file size limit: 40 MB or less." Academic records: 50 MB or less. Activity records: 40 MB or less. Immigration record: 10 MB or less. Activity records are limited to 20 A4 pages and 10 items, and personal portfolios are not accepted.
  • Saramin, Korea's job site, portfolio attachments: up to 50 MB per file and 200 MB in total (checked November 20, 2026).

Some schools take art portfolios only by registered mail, not online (Korea National University of Arts, 2027 undergraduate guide). Check the file count, the limit per file, page limits and the scanning rule (scanner only, or phone photos allowed) in the guide for the year you apply.

1. Scan the documents

  1. If the guide asks for a scanner, use one instead of phone photos.
  2. About 200 dpi keeps text readable. 300 dpi or more makes files much larger.
  3. It's fine if the scanner makes one PDF per document. You merge them in order in the next step.

2. Merge into one PDF in the required order (free)

Finder and Preview can merge too (see how to combine PDFs on Mac). In Dropfit:

  1. Select all the scanned PDFs and photos and drag them onto the "Merge" side of "PDF" on the circle on your desktop.
  2. In the "Merge PDF" window, drag the files into the order the guide lists. The file count and total pages show at the top, so you can check page limits right away.
  3. Type the file name the guide asks for in "Name" and click "Merge." The merged PDF is saved next to the first file, and the originals stay untouched.

3. Fit it under the limit: a Dropfit preset (Dropfit Pro)

Dropfit for Mac is an app I built. Save a submission rule as a preset (a slot with a format and size set) and merging and fitting happen in one go.

  1. Control-click the circle and open Settings. In one slot, set a name (for example, Sangmyung), the PDF format and a size of 50 MB. If each document set has its own limit, as at Soongsil, make one slot per set, such as "Soongsil eligibility · 40 MB" and "Soongsil academic · 50 MB."
  2. Drag all the documents onto the "Merge" side of that slot, set the order in the "Merge PDF" window and click "Merge."
  3. One PDF is saved with "(50MB)" at the end of its name.
  • Photos are compressed so the whole file fits the limit, and PDFs you add keep their text and lines as they are. If the merged file is still too large, the images inside it are compressed once more.
  • If it still can't get under the limit, Dropfit keeps the smallest file it made and tells you it can't get under 50 MB. Go back to step 1 and rescan at a lower resolution.
  • In Dropfit, 1 MB is 1,000,000 bytes. A file fitted to 50 MB stays under the limit even on sites that count a megabyte as 1,048,576 bytes.

Before you upload

  • File count: one PDF, or one per document set?
  • Size: select the file in Finder and press Command-I
  • Order and pages: open it in Preview and check the page order and page limit
  • Text: zoom in and make sure small print such as grades and ID numbers is readable
  • Photo: many schools take the ID photo separately (Sangmyung 2027: JPG, 300 KB or less). See how to resize an ID photo

FAQ

The guide says "one PDF, 50 MB or less." What do I do?

Scan all the documents, merge them into one PDF in the order the guide lists, and check that it is under 50 MB. In Dropfit, dropping the documents on a preset set to PDF and 50 MB merges and fits them in one step.

My scans won't shrink under the limit. Now what?

Rescanning at about 200 dpi is the reliable fix. Files already scanned at 300 dpi or more tend to get blurry text however you shrink them.

Does this work for portfolios too?

For sites that take up to 50 MB per file, like Saramin, yes. But some schools take portfolios only by mail (Korea National University of Arts, 2027), and some tracks don't accept personal portfolios (Soongsil 2027 overseas applicants, activity records), so check the guide first.

Are my original files changed?

No. Dropfit saves the merged PDF as a new file and leaves the originals alone. Nothing is uploaded to the internet.
