How to compress photos on Mac (many at once, to a size you set)

November 2026 · macOS 14 or later

The easiest way to compress a photo on a Mac is the built-in Preview app: choose File → Export, pick JPEG and move the Quality slider left until the estimated size is small enough. For many photos, Finder's Convert Image quick action works on all of them at once, but it can't hold each file under a set size. If every photo has to be under a limit such as 1 MB, an app with a maximum size is quicker.

A form says "JPG, max 1 MB" and you have twenty photos. Online compressors work, but you upload every photo to someone else's server and download them one by one. Your Mac can do it without uploading anything.

1. Preview (free, one or two photos)

  1. Open the photo in Preview.
  2. Choose File → Export.
  3. Pick JPEG and move the Quality slider left. The estimated file size changes as you move it.
  4. Save when it is under your limit.

Stop a little under the limit: very low quality blurs text and faces. Select the saved file and press ⌘I to see its real size.

2. Finder Convert Image (free, many photos)

  1. Select the photos in Finder.
  2. Control-click and choose Quick Actions → Convert Image.
  3. Choose the format and image size, then convert.

Every photo changes at once, but you pick a pixel size, not a file size. You still have to check each result against a limit like 1 MB.

3. Many photos under a set size: Dropfit for Mac

Dropfit for Mac is an app I built. You drop files on a small circle on your desktop and they come out in the format you choose.

  1. Control-click the circle and, in Settings, set a maximum size on the JPG preset (for example, 1 MB). You only do this once.
  2. Drag the photos, or the whole folder, onto the circle and drop them on that preset.
  3. Each photo is saved under that size next to its original. The originals are untouched, and nothing leaves your Mac.

iPhone photos (HEIC) can go in as they are; they become JPG and fit the size in the same step. If a photo can't get under the size you set, Dropfit tells you. You can name each preset, such as "Visa form 1 MB" or "Portfolio 500 KB", and just drop next time.
